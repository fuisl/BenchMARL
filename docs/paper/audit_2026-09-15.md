# Experiment audit and revised research plan

**Date:** 2026-09-15. **Audited checkout:** 0a40a13. **Latest completed control job:** 1216, source snapshot c6ef929.  
**Scope:** audit and recommendations before implementation. No training, planning, collection, or configuration code was changed. Existing artifacts were inspected; small CPU checks reproduced suspected defects. This document is the central evidence and decision record.

## 1. Decision

**Keep the counterfactual multi-agent research question, but reopen the measurement and identification gates before another architecture sweep.**

The current evidence supports a narrower conclusion: relational predictors often have lower error in their own learned latent spaces, but reliable cooperative planning has not been demonstrated. Several failures are real implementation or evaluation problems. They prevent attributing all negative results to dataset coverage, partial observability, or LeWM's regularizer.

The most consequential findings are:

1. **Buzz Wire reward/termination training discards most terminal blocks.** In the development bank's training split, 142/179 correlated-regime terminations and 93/128 independent-regime terminations are excluded, together with their collision penalties.
2. **The goal experiment does not measure the success described in its note.** It records native task success, lacks a goal-reaching threshold, and measures goal distance after terminated batch slots have continued moving.
3. **The readout diagnostic uses mismatched terminal costs.** Simulator truth includes a terminal partial block; its learned-readout comparator drops that block. The claimed diagnosis of an intrinsically inadequate observation is consequently premature.
4. **The C7 “percentage of interaction captured” has a representation confound.** It divides one model's latent error by another model's no-response error. Physical interaction accuracy needs a common target space.
5. **More work has already run than the plan acknowledges.** Job 1216 completed control evaluation, and job 1201 completed 144 width/regularization runs. Neither should be described as pending or unrun.

**Next action after reviewing this report:** repair the measurement contracts, rescore existing checkpoints, and run a small state-information diagnostic. Only then collect targeted interaction data or launch a new model comparison. The proposed repair sequence is in [Section 9](#9-the-next-experiments-in-order).

### Alignment with the intended impact

The [direction](direction.md), [impact notes](multi_agent_world_model_impact_notes.md), and [proposal](multi_agent_latent_mpc_proposal.md) ask whether joint-action effects learned offline support counterfactual decisions. They correctly position VMAS as a source of exact experimental ground truth. Retain that framing.

Revise the working question to:

> **Which state information and interaction-conditioned joint-action coverage make compact world models reliable for ranking and executing cooperative goal plans? Does relational structure reduce the data needed once those interactions are identifiable?**

This retains the multi-agent contribution while allowing a negative architecture result. It also separates a useful representation, identifiable dynamics, and a valid planning objective.

## 3. Implementation findings, ordered by impact

“Confirmed” means the inspected code implements the behavior, with a reproduction where stated. “Risk” means an affected result has not been demonstrated. Neither label asserts that one defect explains the whole experiment.

### F1 — High: terminal supervision is discarded

[Dataset construction, line 186](../../examples/world_model/dataset.py#L186) declares a block valid only if every primitive transition is live. This is reasonable for a fixed-horizon dynamics target, but [readout_losses, lines 115–130](../../examples/world_model/train.py#L115) uses the same mask for block reward and termination. Thus termination at steps 1–4 of a five-step block removes the entire reward/termination example.

| Bank / training regime | Terminal blocks | Excluded | Retained |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Buzz Wire development / correlated | 179 | 142 (79.3%) | 37 |
| Buzz Wire development / independent | 128 | 93 (72.7%) | 35 |
| Buzz Wire second bank / correlated | 191 | 151 (79.1%) | 40 |
| Buzz Wire second bank / independent | 176 | 147 (83.5%) | 29 |

Development training excludes block-summed team rewards of approximately −3060 and −1880 respectively. These are sums over bank snippets, not independent episode returns. Validation has the same bias: only 9/34 and 6/29 terminal blocks survive in the two development regimes.

The collector preserves terminal primitive rewards correctly; the downstream mask loses them. [Collector, lines 121–175](../../examples/world_model/collect.py#L121), [primitive reward aggregation](../../examples/world_model/train.py#L87), [counts and reward sums](../../outputs/audit_20260915/derived_evidence.json), key terminal_mask.

**Consequence:** the readout and its validation underrepresent the events dominating Buzz Wire cost. This affects original and tuned checkpoints. No observed task terminations means this particular failure does not explain Transport or Wheel results.

**Proposed repair contract:** separate dynamics validity from reward/termination validity. Preserve the first terminal reward and use an actual terminal endpoint or a defined absorbing-state representation. Merely changing all() to any() would admit padded/invalid endpoint observations and is not a sufficient fix.

### F2 — High: the readout diagnostic changes the target cost

[simulate](../../examples/world_model/readout_diagnostic.py#L84) includes every live primitive reward, including the terminal transition, in simulator truth. Its block-valid mask requires all five primitives to be live. [readout_costs](../../examples/world_model/readout_diagnostic.py#L107) then drops every incomplete terminal block.

Consequently, the diagnostic labeled “readout error only” excludes penalties retained in its ground truth. In addition, the readout was trained on one-step predicted latents and is evaluated here on encoded true observations; that distribution change is another variable.

The observed Buzz Wire anticorrelation is real for this implementation. **It does not establish that missing ball observations are its sole cause, or that improving dynamics cannot help.** Transport's much better score from true latents is stronger evidence for a rollout/readout-distribution problem because its bank lacks these terminal events. [Raw diagnostic outputs](../../outputs/readout_diag_1203)

### F3 — High: goal success is not implemented as documented

The [closed-loop note](experiments/05_closed_loop_control.md) says goal success is distance-thresholded. In the executed [closed_loop.py, lines 265–295](../../outputs/closed_loop_1216/source/closed_loop.py#L265), every policy uses the same native scenario outcome. Only a distance field is appended.

There is no goal-reaching tolerance, goal-hit latch, or termination-on-goal contract. The saved success column therefore means native task success for **all** policies. Goal reaching is currently unmeasured, rather than proven unsuccessful.

### F4 — High: final goal distance uses post-terminal observations

[EpisodeStats](../../examples/world_model/mpc.py#L87) correctly latches native rewards and outcomes. However, [the batch loop](../../examples/world_model/mpc.py#L281) continues stepping finished slots with zero actions. Zero force does not freeze velocity or constraint dynamics. [closed_loop.py:290](../../examples/world_model/closed_loop.py#L290) reads observations only after the entire batch ends.

A CPU replay of the random policy on the 20 selected Buzz Wire anchors found **18/20 final observations differed from their first-terminal observations**; mean joint-observation drift was 0.09795 and maximum 0.21654. This reproduces the defect on the same anchors, not the exact GPU trajectories.

**Consequence:** saved goal_observation_distance is not reliable as first-terminal goal distance. Native returns/successes remain latched and are not invalidated by this specific bug. [Probe results](../../outputs/audit_20260915/derived_evidence.json), key posttermination.

### F5 — High: generated goal endpoints can lie past termination

[terminal_observations:133](../../examples/world_model/goal_planning.py#L126) takes the number of complete blocks and clamps it to at least one. If termination occurs inside the first block, frame 1 is after termination. If it occurs inside a later block, the function returns an earlier complete-block observation rather than the actual endpoint.

Reconstructing job 1216's goal-action sampling on CPU for Buzz Wire:

- One of 20 goal-source plans had no complete valid block and selected a post-terminal frame.
- Four of 20 selected observations differed from the actual first-terminal/final endpoint.
- The maximum difference was 0.25545 in joint-observation norm.

Earlier complete-block observations can still be reachable. The first-block case breaks the blanket “reachable by construction” claim. This function also affects the open-loop goal prototype. [Probe evidence](../../outputs/audit_20260915/derived_evidence.json), key goal_first_plan.

### F6 — High for mechanism claims: cross-model latent metrics are not a common physical target

[C7 branch scoring](../../examples/world_model/counterfactual_evaluation.py#L62) encodes the target separately with each model. [The reported normalization](../../examples/world_model/counterfactual_evaluation.py#L247) divides all response errors by the independent model's mean no-response error. [Paired response differences](../../examples/world_model/counterfactual_evaluation.py#L266) also subtract errors in different learned spaces.

SIGReg does not ensure that different encoders retain the same physical variables. Therefore “26% of the physical effect captured” and its replication as approximately 50% are too strong. Even with a shared space, one minus an error ratio is a **reduction in squared response error**, not a fraction of displacement or causal mechanism recovered.

A small check of development seed 4100 retains encouraging evidence: using each model's **own** no-response denominator gives independent ratios 1.000/1.000, joint 0.840/0.884, and relational 0.688/0.632 for correlated/independent training. This is one-seed evidence of learned latent response; it does not revalidate eight-seed physical claims. [Checkpoints and exact probe values](../../outputs/audit_20260915/c7_own_floor_probe.json)

**Next metric:** use common physical state changes, signed velocities, object displacement, and intervention response. Model-specific probes may map to these shared targets, but must be fitted on training data and assessed on held-out episodes. Own-encoder normalized response is a useful secondary diagnostic.

### F7 — Medium, confirmed edge case: survival weighting drops the current terminal block

[model_costs:128](../../examples/world_model/plan_ranking.py#L128) computes a cumulative product and divides by the current survival factor. If the termination sigmoid rounds to 1, the numerator is zero and the clamped denominator cannot recover the required prefix product.

For termination probabilities [1, 0.5, 0.5], current weights are [0, 0, 0]; the correct weights at block starts are [1, 0, 0]. A direct exclusive prefix product avoids this failure. [Numeric reproduction](../../outputs/audit_20260915/derived_evidence.json), key survival.

**Impact on completed runs is unquantified:** the reproduction establishes a defect, not that their logits reached this regime.

### F8 — Medium: planning contracts and history are confounded

- [closed_loop.py:231](../../examples/world_model/closed_loop.py#L231) sets executed blocks equal to the planning horizon. At H=5 this executes 25 primitive steps before receiving new feedback. [shift_plan](../../examples/world_model/mpc.py#L41) then retains no unused actions, so the enabled warm start contains zeros.
- Goal scoring uses a three-frame truncated predicted history; reward scoring uses the growing rollout context. Comparing them changes both the objective and rollout history.
- Each decision starts from one current observation. Truncating predicted history does not supply the missing history of actually observed states/actions.
- The oracle baseline optimizes native reward. It is not the appropriate goal-objective oracle, and it is not a guaranteed global optimum.

These are mostly experimental choices, not reversed-action bugs. Report them explicitly and separate execution cadence from horizon in the next pilot. [Reward scorer](../../examples/world_model/plan_ranking.py#L113), [goal scorer](../../examples/world_model/goal_planning.py#L48), [CEM final-mean selection](../../examples/world_model/cem.py#L151).

### F9 — Medium: uncertainty overstates the independent sample count

The 20 selected anchors in each task come from **10 root episodes**, with repeated anchors/states across policies and seeds. [summarize](../../examples/world_model/closed_loop.py#L148) bootstraps anchor rows as independent; Wilson intervals have the analogous independence limitation. Training seeds quantify optimization variation conditional on a bank, not data-collection uncertainty.

Use paired comparisons on identical anchors; resample root episodes as clusters and training seeds separately. Report bank replication separately. With only ten root clusters, intervals are necessarily fragile. Native-success counts should stay descriptive until evaluation is expanded with independent roots.

Current [report.aggregate](../../examples/world_model/report.py#L69) also flattens seeds × tasks before bootstrapping. Preserve task strata for a fixed benchmark and keep paired seed structure where appropriate. Aggregate normalized latent error is not an aggregate physical-control score.

### F10 — Medium: proof-of-validation labels exceed the checks

- [verify_reload:506](../../examples/world_model/train.py#L504) loads the same checkpoint twice. A zero difference verifies deterministic reconstruction; it does not compare the trained in-memory model with its saved/reloaded version.
- [termination_head_validated:417](../../examples/world_model/train.py#L410) is true whenever validation contains a positive termination target. It is not a calibration, recall, or planning-validity test.
- Same training seed does not give identical predictor initialization across architectures: the differently sized conditioner is constructed before the predictor and consumes different random numbers. Shared data order remains useful; “identical initialization” needs narrowing. [Construction order](../../examples/world_model/models.py#L344)

These weaken validation claims without demonstrating corrupted checkpoint weights.

### F11 — Medium risk: cached truth and task reconstruction are insufficiently checked

[plan_ranking.py:176](../../examples/world_model/plan_ranking.py#L176) accepts an existing cache without validating dataset hash, task/configuration, candidate seed, horizon, or code version. The default cache name is task-agnostic. Explicit per-job cache paths reduce this risk in recorded runs; no actual cross-task cache contamination was established.

[true_costs:82](../../examples/world_model/plan_ranking.py#L82) and [closed_loop.py:222](../../examples/world_model/closed_loop.py#L222) reconstruct task YAML defaults from the task name rather than applying the manifest's resolved task configuration. This can invalidate future task overrides or replay after defaults change. Validate configuration identity before reusing a bank.

### F12 — Documentation and reproducibility need a single authoritative status

The closed-loop note still says pending, the M4 note says the SIGReg alternatives are unrun, and some scripts retain older success/runtime descriptions. The current reporting implementation differs from the one that produced some files under outputs/report. ## 4. Are the experiments being conducted correctly?

**Several foundations are sound; the end-to-end scientific claim is not yet validated.**

What should be retained:

- Exact shared anchor snapshots and paired initial actions make the first-block coverage contrast interpretable.
- Root episodes are split before branching, and train-only normalization is implemented. Checksums and contiguous-transition checks are valuable.
- Replay checks distinguish physical effects from changes caused merely by relative observation coordinates.
- Three architecture families share data and training budgets, with closely matched dynamics parameter counts within each configured width.
- The native oracle/controller statistics latch the first terminal outcome and reward.
- Failed runs, source snapshots, resolved configs, and negative results have largely been retained.

Evidence: [Transport leakage/replay checks](../../outputs/transport_data_1190/leakage_checks.json), [Buzz Wire checks](../../outputs/buzz_wire_1196/data/leakage_checks.json), [Wheel checks](../../outputs/wheel_1205/data/leakage_checks.json), [dataset reader](../../examples/world_model/dataset.py), [training loader and normalization](../../examples/world_model/train.py#L48).

What needs correction in interpretation:

- Matched starting states do not make later branch-state distributions identical.
- Uniform individual action marginals do not establish conditional joint-action coverage.
- Non-collapse and low latent MSE do not establish useful action sensitivity or goal geometry.
- A null result on one intervention axis/horizon does not establish absence of interaction.
- Test anchors used repeatedly to choose the next hypothesis have become development evidence for those choices. This is adaptive evaluation, not necessarily training leakage. Confirm the next selected hypothesis on new roots/banks.
- Eight optimization seeds on one bank do not replace independent data collection.

### Tests do not currently cover the discovered scientific failures

During this audit, **50 existing tests passed in 9.67 seconds** on CPU across dataset, model, goal, and closed-loop test modules. No new tests or fixes were added. [Exact output](../../outputs/audit_20260915/existing_tests.txt)

The goal endpoint test covers one or more complete blocks, but not zero complete blocks. Dataset tests preserve a partial block's primitive reward without checking that readout training consumes it. This explains how a passing suite can coexist with the observed defects.

## 6. What “sufficient coverage” should mean here

Define sufficiency **relative to a decision problem**: a start-state distribution, goal family, action bounds, horizon, and tolerated decision error. No finite bank can certify all continuous VMAS dynamics.

LeWM assumes informative offline observation/action trajectories, uses a two-term prediction/SIGReg objective, and plans toward a goal embedding. Its paper does not supply a quantitative dataset-coverage certificate or guarantee successful planning from arbitrary logs. It also discusses low-diversity, low-intrinsic-dimensionality data as a possible source of difficulty, and constructs reachable start/goal pairs from trajectories. [LeWM, §§3–4 and Appendix F](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.19312v1)

Reward-free exploration theory gives a more precise precedent: in finite-horizon tabular MDPs, suitable visitation coverage and sufficient sampling permit planning for subsequently supplied bounded rewards. Those assumptions do not automatically hold for continuous, partially observed, neural multi-agent models. Treating a cooperative team as centralized makes its action the **joint action**, which increases the coverage burden. [Jin et al., Theorems 3.3 and 3.5](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/jin20d/jin20d.pdf)

### 6.1 Why marginal coverage is insufficient

An illustrative identifiability counterexample:

~~~text
Dynamics 1: next = a1 + a2
Dynamics 2: next = 2*a1

On data where a1 = a2, both fit perfectly.
After recombining actions, they disagree.
~~~

This is an example, not an exact description of our sign-correlated collector. Our [collector](../../examples/world_model/collect.py#L95) preserves uniform magnitudes while sharing signs across agents, deliberately excluding portions of joint-action support. Architecture supplies a preference among extrapolations; it cannot prove the unobserved one correct.

### 6.2 Replace transition counts with a coverage scorecard

| Coverage dimension | Measurement | What would count as progress |
|---|---|---|
| State/contact | Roots and anchors in approach, contact, coordinated motion, collision/recovery, near-goal states | Adequate independent roots in the states queried by planning |
| Joint action conditional on state | Action variation and held-out combinations within each contact stratum | Same marginals and state anchors, progressively broader joint support |
| Effect directions | Change each agent and action axis; include paired coordinated deviations | Detectable effects spanning the task-relevant movement directions |
| Time scale | Responses at 1, 5, 10, 25 primitive steps; report censoring | Training/prediction horizon includes the interaction's onset |
| Outcomes | Reachable goal neighborhoods, failures, and partial terminal blocks | Useful goal transitions and retained terminal events |
| Planner distribution | Support and true errors for random candidates, initial CEM proposals, later elites, and selected plans | Reliability survives optimization-induced distribution shift |
| Data scaling | Small/medium/large banks with matched collection protocol | Decision error and control improve reproducibly with added support |

A single-agent intervention is not sufficient to expose every coordination mechanism. Add the mixed difference of two agents' interventions, holding the same simulator state fixed:

~~~text
joint interaction =
    next(ai_changed, aj_changed)
  - next(ai_changed, aj_original)
  - next(ai_original, aj_changed)
  + next(ai_original, aj_original)
~~~

Measure it in shared physical coordinates and for relevant object motion. This can expose effects that require coordinated actions. Related multi-agent search work uses single and paired deviations; its search guarantees do not transfer to our CEM. [NonZero](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.00751v1)

### 6.3 Collection revision

Preserve shared anchors and episode splits. Obtain more anchors from trajectories that actually reach useful contacts, using the existing heuristic or a competent cooperative controller where available. At those anchors, branch into matched correlated, individual-intervention, paired-intervention, and broader independent actions.

Hold transition budgets, starting-state distribution, and per-agent marginals fixed in the coverage comparison. Later branch divergence is an outcome to report. A mixture of competent behavior and local perturbations is more informative than adding many free-space random steps. Fit support summaries on training data; keep the final evaluation root bank untouched.

Do not call behavior generated by a task-aware heuristic “task-agnostic exploration.” A dynamics loss can still be reward-free on that bank; disclose how its trajectories were collected.

## 7. Better representations and theory-informed tuning

### 7.1 Diagnose missing information before choosing a new architecture

Use the same joint predictor first, under three input conditions:

1. Current observations and current actual-history interface.
2. Joint observation/action history, with a small fixed window of **real** past observations.
3. Minimal simulator state needed for the relevant physical transition, explicitly labeled a privileged diagnostic.

The third condition should include dynamic shared bodies and signed velocities/angular velocities. For Buzz Wire include ball state; for Transport include package state; for Wheel include line angle and signed angular velocity. Time-to-timeout belongs in terminal-cost bookkeeping. Verify Markov sufficiency rather than assuming a snapshot-derived subset is complete.

Start with fixed normalized state features or a simple residual next-state model as a diagnostic. This removes representation learning as a variable. Compare against persistence and own-action dynamics on state **changes**, not only next-state levels.

Once information sufficiency is established, compare joint and relational predictors with identical information and shared physical evaluation targets. An entity representation with agent and shared-object tokens is a motivated candidate, not a proven fix. Objects and relational world models are established ideas. [C-SWM](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.12247), [CoDreamer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.13600)

### 7.2 Correct the SIGReg argument

The M4 note's claim that low linear input rank imposes the same maximum nonlinear latent covariance rank is incorrect. A scalar x mapped to (x, x²) can have rank-two covariance. Smooth expansion of an intrinsically lower-dimensional distribution cannot produce an exact full-dimensional Gaussian density; that is a different statement from a finite projected-test floor.

A 330:1 ratio of objective values also does not establish that regularization dominates gradients. The relevant diagnostics are encoder gradient norms from each loss, their alignment, representation variance/effective rank, and action-response fidelity.

LeJEPA's distribution/probing theory depends on its stated statistical and representation assumptions. Matching finitely many projections after finite optimization is not a guarantee of control-relevant information, calibrated dynamics, or joint-action extrapolation. [LeJEPA, theory and SIGReg sections](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08544v1)

### 7.3 Multi-agent tuning protocol

- **Reuse the six completed settings first.** Rescore the 192-dimensional, 0.009/0.09 checkpoints using corrected decision metrics before training another width sweep.
- **Make sample scaling explicit.** Current [dynamics_losses](../../examples/world_model/train.py#L99) pools valid frames and agents into the SIGReg population. Neighboring frames and linked agents are dependent; changing their count changes an N-scaled test statistic. Compare a fixed sampling/reduction convention, rather than assuming the same weight means the same regularization pressure at every team size.
- **Separate capacity from width.** Report parameter counts and maintain a matched-capacity comparison if width is the scientific variable.
- **Select on validation decision quality.** Use common-space intervention error, goal-plan regret, and representation diagnostics. Do not select a model because its own latent MSE or effective rank alone is lowest/highest.
- **Verify projector/normalization semantics against the recorded reference revision.** The local vector MLP is a deliberate adaptation; retain a documented reference-compatible control.
- **Only add multi-step supervision if rollout is the remaining bottleneck.** Keep the same input/data/objective, match update and compute budgets, and assess short-horizon versus long-horizon error.

Published options already include subspace Gaussian regularization and multi-horizon action-prefix prediction. Use them as attributed baselines if the corresponding bottleneck survives the audit. [Sub-JEPA](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.09241v1), [Fast LeWorldModel](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26217v1)

## 8. A clean reward-free, goal-based planning experiment

The current reward branch trains the representation without reward, then uses reward-supervised heads for planning. Describe it that way. A separate goal branch can avoid reward/termination heads, while still requiring an explicit goal and evaluation semantics.

### Required contract

1. Construct **jointly reachable** start/goal pairs from one held-out simulator trajectory. Do not independently combine agent goal observations that may violate shared-body constraints.
2. Preserve the goal observation, source actions, actual endpoint, horizon, root identity, and terminal status.
3. Define whether success means reaching and holding a full configuration, reaching a shared-object target, or matching velocity. Specify units and tolerance before evaluating models.
4. Latch goal hit, native success, collision, and first-terminal endpoint separately. Goals requiring velocity should be checked at the required time or with a dwell criterion.
5. Validate simulator MPC on the **same goal objective**. Include random and zero-action/persistence baselines; short random-trajectory goals can be close to the initial state.
6. Use identical candidate plans and execution cadence across models.

Matching the entire goal observation may overconstrain irrelevant agent placement. Begin with a clearly defined full-configuration goal benchmark. Object-focused, permutation-invariant, or task-variable goals are later objective variants and should be labeled as such.

**Reward-free does not remove feasibility or termination.** A predictor trained mainly on nonterminal blocks can invent a trajectory through a collision to a nearby goal. Specify how terminal futures are represented and scored, using the same convention for simulator and learned evaluation. An absorbing-outcome representation or a learned feasibility signal is a possible later choice; disclose any termination supervision. Keep native collision outcomes visible even when the goal cost itself uses no reward head, and never supply simulator feasibility information secretly to the learned planner.

### Decompose the error on the same candidate set

| Score | Dynamics | Goal metric | Question |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Simulator | Declared physical goal distance | Is the goal/search problem meaningful? |
| B | Simulator | Encode true endpoint and goal, then latent distance | Does the learned representation order meaningful goals? |
| C | Learned model | Predicted latent endpoint distance | What extra error does learned rollout introduce? |

Compare A–B for representation/cost geometry and B–C for prediction. Report selected-plan regret, top-plan agreement, and physical endpoint error, with rankable fraction. Then execute the selected plans in closed loop. This is a more diagnostic continuation of job 1204 than comparing its Spearman number directly with native reward ranking.

Offline feature-goal MPC has precedents in [DINO-WM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.04983) and object-centric [Slot-MPC](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.14937v1). The multi-agent study needs to demonstrate what joint-action coverage and interaction structure add.

### A useful guarantee, with explicit premises

**Elementary derivation for this report:** for a fixed candidate set, suppose every predicted cost differs from its true cost by at most epsilon, and search returns a plan within eta of the minimum predicted cost. Then:

~~~text
true selected-plan regret <= 2*epsilon + eta.

J(selected) <= J_hat(selected) + epsilon
            <= min_A J_hat(A) + eta + epsilon
            <= min_A J(A) + eta + 2*epsilon.
~~~

This is a conditional algebraic bound, not an empirical guarantee for our checkpoints or CEM. It motivates measuring errors on actual selected plans and search quality. Average logged latent MSE does not establish its uniform-cost-error premise. CEM's returned elite mean may not be a previously evaluated candidate, so include its cost when applying this reasoning.

- Reuse cached simulator truth only after verifying its identity and terminal semantics.
- Measure a tiny representative workload on the intended allocation; estimate total decisions and candidate transitions before submission.
- Report simulator evaluation time separately from learned planning latency, and batch throughput separately from per-environment latency.

## 10. Literature and the plausible contribution

| Primary work | What can be borrowed | What it does not establish here |
|---|---|---|
| [LeWM](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.19312v1) | Simple reward-free latent training and goal MPC | Coverage, successful control, or optimal CEM search on this bank |
| [LeJEPA](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08544v1) | Principled representation regularization and its assumptions | Physical sufficiency of finite learned MA embeddings |
| [Reward-Free Exploration](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/jin20d.html) | Formal connection between adequate visitation coverage and subsequent planning | A certificate for continuous partially observed offline VMAS |
| [C-SWM](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.12247), [CoDreamer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.13600) | Entity structure and modeling inter-agent information | Novelty of relational MA world modeling itself |
| [Sub-JEPA](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.09241v1) | Low-dimensional subspace regularization | A guaranteed fix for missing interactions |
| [Fast LeWM](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26217v1) | Action-prefix/multi-horizon prediction | A guarantee that unsupported joint actions become predictable |
| [Implicit Causal World Models, July 2026 technical report](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.26336v1) | Controlled intervention strength, observation comparisons, and conditional mechanism analysis | First offline causal MA model as our novelty claim |

The last paper is particularly close to this direction. It reports that conditioning on intervention strength changes apparent relationships between causal-graph quality and prediction, warns that indiscriminate random actions can destroy useful cooperation, and leaves downstream behavioral validation as further work. Its identification assumptions are not automatically satisfied by our local observations.

**A credible contribution would be a controlled finding that interaction-conditioned coverage plus sufficient state information improves decision-relevant joint-action prediction, and showing when relational models need fewer such data.** The valuable artifact could include a reproducible intervention/goal-planning benchmark and a failure decomposition, even if a simpler joint model ultimately wins.

Do not claim a breakthrough, first method, or universal guarantee before that evidence exists. Narrow the paper around the measured link from offline interventions to decisions.
